# Steakhouse Steak Sandwich (Reverse-Seared Ribeye)

This is a steakhouse-lunch steak sandwich, not a cheesesteak: a thick, reverse-seared boneless ribeye, edge-to-edge medium-rare, sliced against the grain onto a mayo-toasted baguette with creamy horseradish sauce. Serves 2.

## Ingredients

**Steak**
- 1 boneless ribeye, ~1¼–1½ in thick (a really nice one; this one was from Whole Foods)
- Dry rub, enough to coat all sides
- High smoke point oil (avocado oil)

**Bread**
- 1 fresh baguette, cut lengthwise
- Mayonnaise, a light even coat on the cut sides
- Flaky finishing salt

**Creamy horseradish sauce**
- ½ cup sour cream (swap a spoonful for mayo if you want it richer)
- 2–3 tbsp prepared horseradish
- 1 tsp Dijon mustard
- Squeeze of lemon juice
- Salt and pepper

## Steps

1. **Sauce:** Mix the sour cream, horseradish, Dijon, lemon, salt and pepper. Refrigerate at least 30 min so the flavours come together.
2. **Rub and rest:** Coat the ribeye in the dry rub and put it back in the fridge for a while, uncovered.
3. **Low oven:** Set the steak on a wire rack over a sheet pan so air flows all the way around it. Cook in a low oven (typically ~250 °F) until it's about 10–15 °F below your target (pull ~115–120 °F for medium-rare-plus).
4. **Toast the bread:** Meanwhile, spread a light, even layer of mayo on the cut sides of the baguette and brown it cut side down in a pan. Sprinkle with finishing salt right after.
5. **Hard sear:** Get a cast iron pan smoking hot with a little avocado oil. Sear the steak hard on both sides (and the fat edge) without moving it, until there's a dark crust.
6. **Rest and slice:** Rest ~5 min, then slice against the grain, a bit thicker than cheesesteak slices.
7. **Build:** Horseradish cream on the toasted baguette, then the sliced steak.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Why reverse sear:** The low oven cooks the steak gently and evenly, so you get edge-to-edge pink with no grey band. The pan is only for crust.
- **Wire rack matters:** Airflow on all sides means even cooking and a drier surface going into the pan.
- **Mayo, not butter, on the bread:** It browns more evenly and doesn't burn as easily.
- **Slice against the grain:** Each bite stays tender and the steak doesn't pull out of the sandwich in one chewy strip.
- **Steakhouse vs. cheesesteak:** Steakhouse versions use crusty bread (baguette, ciabatta, garlic toast), a proper cut cooked medium-rare, and horseradish cream or au jus. Common extras: rocket, crispy fried onions, blue cheese, steak butter.

## Next Steps

Ideas for a deeper, steakhouse-level crust, inspired by Bavette's and Boeufhaus in Chicago.

### 1. Buy dry-aged beef
- **What:** Get a dry-aged ribeye or strip (a good butcher, or Whole Foods' dry-aged case if there is one).
- **Why:** This is a big part of what the top Chicago places do. Boeufhaus uses prime, mostly dry-aged beef (their 55-day dry-aged KC strip comes out with a firm crust and crimson centre, topped with flaky salt and chives). The drier surface crusts harder and the flavour is far beefier.

### 2. Simplify the seasoning
- **What:** Try coarse salt and cracked black pepper only, instead of the rub.
- **Why:** Rubs with sugar or garlic powder can burn and turn bitter before a deep crust forms, so you end up pulling the steak early. Check whether the current rub has sugar in it.

### 3. Go hotter (Chicago-style char)
- **What:** Aim for an aggressive, near-black crust while the inside stays rare. Steakhouse broilers run much hotter than a home pan, so get cast iron properly smoking, or use a grill turned all the way up.

### 4. Butter baste
- **What:** In the last minute of the sear, add butter, garlic and thyme and spoon the foaming butter over the steak.

### 5. Overnight dry brine
- **What:** Salt the steak and leave it uncovered on the rack in the fridge overnight before cooking.

### Plan for next time
- **Day before:** Dry-aged ribeye, salt (and pepper) only, uncovered on a rack in the fridge.
- **Cook day:** Low oven to ~10–15 °F under target, then the hottest sear you can manage plus a butter baste. Same mayo-toasted baguette and horseradish cream.
