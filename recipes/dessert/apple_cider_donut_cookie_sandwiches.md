# Apple Cider Donut Cookie Sandwiches (with Salted Caramel)

All the cozy, spiced flavour of a fresh-from-the-orchard apple cider cake donut transformed into a chewy, cinnamon-sugar-crusted cookie sandwich. The secret is reducing fresh orchard apple cider down to a concentrated, syrupy glaze so you pack intense tangy apple flavour into the dough without making it cakey or wet, then sandwiching two cookies around a ring of salted caramel buttercream with a gooey salted caramel core. Makes ~10–12 cookie sandwiches (20–24 individual cookies). About 1½ hours (including cider reduction and dough chill).

## Ingredients

**Reduced apple cider (the flavour booster)**
- 4–6 cups (1–1.4 L) fresh orchard apple cider (unfiltered, spiced or plain—not clear apple juice), reduced down to **½ cup (120 ml)**

**Spiced apple cider cookies**
- 2½ cups (300 g) all-purpose flour
- 1 tsp baking soda
- ¼ tsp baking powder
- 1½ tsp ground cinnamon
- ½ tsp freshly grated nutmeg (essential for that unmistakable cake-donut flavour)
- ¼ tsp ground allspice (or pinch of ground cloves/cardamom)
- ½ tsp fine sea salt
- ¾ cup (170 g / 1½ sticks) unsalted butter, softened to room temperature
- ½ cup (100 g) granulated sugar
- ½ cup (100 g) packed dark brown sugar
- 1 large egg + 1 large egg yolk, room temperature
- 2 tsp pure vanilla extract
- ½ cup (120 ml) cooled reduced apple cider (from above)

**Cinnamon-sugar coating**
- ⅓ cup (67 g) granulated sugar
- 1 tsp ground cinnamon

**Salted caramel buttercream & core**
- ½ cup (113 g / 1 stick) unsalted butter, softened to room temperature
- 1½–2 cups (180–240 g) powdered sugar, sifted
- ½ cup thick salted caramel sauce, divided (3–4 tbsp for the buttercream + the rest for the centre wells)
- 1 tsp pure vanilla extract
- ½ tsp flaky sea salt (like Maldon), plus extra for finishing

## Steps

1. **Reduce the apple cider:** Pour the apple cider into a wide saucepan or skillet (more surface area = faster evaporation). Bring to a boil over medium-high heat, then simmer briskly for 30–45 min, stirring occasionally near the end so it doesn't scorch, until reduced to **exactly ½ cup (120 ml)**. It should be dark amber and syrupy, like warm maple syrup. Pour into a heatproof measuring cup and **cool completely to room temperature** (20–30 min in the fridge works great).
2. **Whisk the dry ingredients:** In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, baking soda, baking powder, cinnamon, nutmeg, allspice and salt.
3. **Cream the butter and sugars:** In a stand mixer fitted with the paddle attachment (or with a hand mixer), beat the softened butter, granulated sugar and dark brown sugar on medium-high speed for ~2 min until light and fluffy.
4. **Add the wet ingredients:** Beat in the whole egg, egg yolk and vanilla until smooth. Pour in the **½ cup cooled reduced apple cider** and mix until fully incorporated (if it looks slightly curdled from the cider's acidity, don't worry—the flour brings it right back together).
5. **Fold and chill:** Mix in the dry ingredients on low speed just until no flour streaks remain. Cover the bowl and **refrigerate the dough for 45–60 min** until firm enough to scoop cleanly.
6. **Prep and roll:** Preheat the oven to 350 °F (175 °C) and line two large baking sheets with parchment paper. Stir together the ⅓ cup granulated sugar and 1 tsp cinnamon in a shallow bowl. Scoop uniform 1½-tablespoon balls of chilled dough (a #40 cookie scoop keeps the sandwich halves identical) and roll generously in the cinnamon sugar.
7. **Bake:** Space the dough balls ~2 inches apart on the sheets and bake for 9–11 min. Pull them when the edges are set and lightly golden, but the centres still look soft and slightly underbaked.
8. **Shape and cool:** Immediately out of the oven, optional pro move: place a large round biscuit cutter or wide glass over each hot cookie and swirl it gently in a circle to scoot the edges into uniform rounds. Cool on the pan for 5 min to set, then transfer to a wire rack to cool completely.
9. **Whip the buttercream:** Beat the ½ cup softened butter on medium speed for 2 min until creamy and pale. Gradually add the powdered sugar along with 3–4 tbsp of the salted caramel sauce, the vanilla and the flaky sea salt, beating until fluffy and pipeable. Transfer to a piping bag (or zip-top bag with the corner snipped).
10. **Build the sandwiches:** Pair up cooled cookies by size and flip half of them flat-side up. Pipe a sturdy ring of salted caramel buttercream around the outer perimeter of each bottom cookie, leaving an empty well in the middle. Spoon ~1 tsp of pure thick salted caramel sauce into the centre well, sprinkle with a few flakes of sea salt, and press the top cookie gently on top until the filling reaches the edges.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Why you must reduce the cider:** Raw apple cider is mostly water—adding ½ cup straight to cookie dough makes cakey, bland cookies that spread too much. Boiling 4–6 cups down to ½ cup evaporates the water and leaves behind concentrated apple sugars, pectin and malic acid (basically homemade *boiled cider*).
- **Cool the reduction completely:** Pouring hot or warm cider syrup into creamed butter melts the butter emulsion instantly, leading to flat, greasy cookies.
- **Freshly grated nutmeg is the "donut shop" secret:** Cinnamon reads as "snickerdoodle," but freshly grated whole nutmeg + allspice is what makes your brain immediately taste a cake donut from an orchard.
- **The buttercream dam trick:** If you spread pure caramel edge-to-edge, it squeezes out the sides on the first bite. Piping a ring of buttercream acts as a structural dam that holds the gooey liquid caramel in the centre.
- **Why the extra egg yolk:** Since reduced cider adds liquid sugar and moisture, replacing a second whole egg white with just the yolk adds fat and lecithin for a fudgy, chewy centre rather than a puffy, bready cookie.
- **Storage & serving:** Store assembled sandwiches in an airtight container in the fridge for up to 4–5 days. Let them sit at room temperature for 10–15 min before eating so the buttercream softens and the cookie texture relaxes.

## Next Steps

Ideas for dialing in the orchard-donut texture or upgrading the filling on future runs.

### 1. Brown the butter for the cookies (or the frosting)
- **What:** Brown the ¾ cup cookie butter in a skillet until nutty and toasted, then chill it back to a scoopable, softened solid before creaming with the sugars (or brown the ½ cup butter for the buttercream).
- **Why:** Brown butter + boiled apple cider + dark brown sugar takes the caramelised, toasted-donut flavour profile over the top and cuts the sweetness of the buttercream.

### 2. Brushed melted-butter "donut dip" finish
- **What:** Right after the cookies come out of the oven, lightly brush the tops of the top-half cookies with melted butter and sprinkle with an extra dusting of cinnamon sugar.
- **Why:** Mimics how warm apple cider donuts are dipped in melted butter and dredged in cinnamon sugar fresh out of the fryer, giving a thicker, crunchier sugar crust.

### 3. Freeze-dried apple powder boost
- **What:** Blitz 1 bag (~1 oz / 30 g) of freeze-dried apples into a fine powder in a spice grinder or food processor and whisk it into the dry flour mixture (or into the cinnamon-sugar coating).
- **Why:** Adds another layer of bright, tart apple punch with zero extra moisture.

### 4. Apple butter + salted caramel swirl core
- **What:** Fill the centre well inside the buttercream ring with ½ tsp spiced apple butter and ½ tsp salted caramel sauce instead of caramel alone.
- **Why:** Doubles down on the orchard fruit flavour and cuts the richness of the buttercream and caramel.
