# Hooters Classic Wings (Air Fryer)

These are Hooters classic wings: hand-breaded, air fried, tossed in buffalo sauce.

## Ingredients

**Wings**
- 2 lb chicken wings
- ~1 tsp salt (for dry-brining)
- Avocado or canola oil spray (100% oil, propellant-free)

**Breading**
- 1 cup flour
- 1 tsp paprika
- 1 tsp garlic powder
- ½ tsp cayenne
- ½ tsp black pepper
- No salt (wings are already salted)

**Buffalo sauce**
- ½ cup butter (1 stick)
- ⅔ cup Frank's RedHot
- Splash of Worcestershire
- Pinch of garlic powder

**To serve**
- Blue cheese dressing (celery optional)

## Steps

1. **Dry-brine:** Pat wings very dry, salt them, and set on a rack in the fridge, uncovered, for a few hours or overnight.
2. **Breading:** Whisk the flour and spices together.
3. **Coat:** Pat wings dry again, toss in breading, shake off excess, and rest ~10 min so the coating sets. Preheat air fryer to 380 °F on **Air Fry**.
4. **Oil:** Put wings in the basket and spray the tops with oil right before cooking.
5. **Cook:** Air fry at 380 °F for 20 min. At 10 min, flip, then spray the other side and any dry floury patches.
6. **Crisp:** Raise to 400 °F for 5–8 min, until deep golden and crisp.
7. **Sauce:** Melt butter in the microwave, whisk in Frank's, Worcestershire and garlic powder.
8. **Toss and serve:** Rewarm sauce ~10 sec, toss wings in a big bowl, and serve right away.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Why it works (the double punch):** The dry-brine salts the chicken all the way through, so the flavour isn't riding on the breading alone. On top of that, the chicken is better quality than what Hooters uses, so the result beats the restaurant version.
- **Oil timing:** Spray right before cooking, never ahead, or the coating goes soggy.
- **Lots of sizzling is normal:** That's the fat rendering out. Only worry if there's real smoke; then pour off the grease.
- **Visual cues:** Pale and floury early on is fine. They're done when deep golden with no white flour patches. Ideal internal temp is 165–175 °F.
- **Butter:** Cut into chunks, cover with a paper towel, and microwave in short bursts. It took about 5 rounds of 25 sec.
- **Don't boil the sauce:** The butter can separate.
- **Resting:** A few minutes is fine, but take wings out of the closed basket so steam doesn't soften them. Sauce right before eating.
- **Measuring:** 1 stick = ½ cup butter. For ⅔ cup Frank's, pull the top off the bottle so it pours fast, and eyeball it.
- **Setup:** Have a wooden board or trivet for the hot basket, the sauce, a big tossing bowl, tongs and the serving plate ready before the wings finish.

## Next Steps

Ideas for taking these further, mostly by getting the skin drier before it cooks.

### 1. Dry-brine longer
- **What:** Salt the wings (~1 tsp per 2 lb) and leave them uncovered on a rack over a tray in the fridge.
- **Time:** 24 hours is the target; up to 48 hours for even drier, papery skin.
- **Limit:** Don't go past ~3 days. The skin can turn leathery instead of crisp, exposed meat dries out, wings pick up fridge smells, and raw chicken is best cooked within a couple of days anyway.

### 2. Add a fan in the fridge
- **What:** Put a small fan (a clip-on or USB fan works) inside the fridge, pointed across the rack so air moves over the wings.
- **Time:** Moving air speeds up drying a lot, so roughly 12–24 hours with a fan should get close to what 24–48 hours gets without one. Check the skin: it's ready when it looks matte, slightly translucent and feels dry and tight to the touch.
- **Food safety:** The fan only goes in the fridge. Don't dry wings in front of a fan at room temperature for more than ~30 minutes.

### 3. Optional: baking powder in the dry brine
- **What:** Mix ~1 tsp baking powder (not baking soda) in with the salt for the dry brine.
- **Why:** It raises the skin's pH so it browns and blisters more, and it still works under the breading.

### Breading drier wings
- Super-dry skin gives the flour less to grab onto. After tossing in the breading, let the wings sit ~10 minutes so the coating hydrates before spraying with oil and cooking.

### Future alternative: buttermilk marinade
- **What:** Instead of the dry brine, marinate the wings in buttermilk with ~1 tsp salt and a good splash of Frank's RedHot.
- **Time:** 4–12 hours in the fridge, covered.
- **Why:** The buttermilk tenderises the meat, and its tackiness helps the breading stick. You get a craggier, crunchier crust instead of the papery, dry-brined skin.
- **How:** Let the excess drip off, dredge straight into the flour mix, rest ~10 min, and spray well with oil on all sides so the breading doesn't come out chalky.

### Plan for next time
- **Day before (or two):** Salt (plus baking powder, if using), rack them uncovered in the fridge, fan on if you have one.
- **Cook day:** Bread, rest 10 min, oil right before cooking, then follow the Steps above.
