# Spaghetti & Small Meatballs in Marcella's Tomato Butter Sauce

Marcella Hazan's famous three-ingredient sauce (tomatoes, butter, a halved onion), doubled so it can braise small pork-and-beef meatballs. The meatballs are roasted hard, then finished in the sauce so they soak it up and make it meatier. The butter emulsifies into the tomato, so it eats silky and almost creamy with no cream. Serves 4–6. About 1¼ hours, most of it hands-off simmering.

## Ingredients

**Sauce (double batch)**
- 2 cans (28 oz / ~800 g each) whole peeled tomatoes (San Marzano if you can), with juices
- 10 tbsp (~140 g) unsalted butter
- 2 medium yellow onions, peeled and halved
- Kosher salt

**Panade**
- 2 slices white sandwich bread, crusts off, torn (or ¾ cup fresh breadcrumbs, not dry)
- ⅓ cup (~80 ml) whole milk

**Meatballs (~40–50 small)**
- 1 lb (~450 g) ground beef (80/20)
- ½ lb (~225 g) ground pork
- 1 egg
- ½ cup (~1.5 oz) Parmigiano-Reggiano, finely grated
- 2 garlic cloves, microplaned
- Small handful flat-leaf parsley, finely chopped
- 1½ tsp kosher salt
- Plenty of black pepper, pinch of nutmeg
- Optional: ⅓ cup whole-milk ricotta, for extra tenderness
- Olive oil, for the sheet pan

**Pasta**
- 1 lb (~450 g) spaghetti
- Kosher salt, for the water

**To finish**
- Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated, for the table
- Optional: 1–2 balls burrata, torn over the bowls

## Steps

1. **Start the sauce:** In a wide, heavy pot, combine the tomatoes with their juices, the butter, the onion halves (cut side down) and a good pinch of salt. Bring to a gentle simmer, then cook uncovered ~45 min on low, stirring now and then and crushing the tomatoes against the pot with a wooden spoon.
2. **Make the panade:** In a large bowl, soak the bread in the milk until fully mushy, ~5 min. Mash it into a paste with the egg, Parmesan, garlic, parsley, salt, pepper, nutmeg (and ricotta if using).
3. **Add the meat:** Add the beef and pork and mix gently with your hands or a fork, just until it comes together. Stop as soon as it looks uniform.
4. **Test and season:** Fry a small spoonful in a pan, taste, and adjust the salt in the mix.
5. **Roll:** With wet hands, roll into ~1-inch balls (a scant tbsp, ~20 g). A small cookie scoop speeds this up. Set them on an oiled sheet pan.
6. **Chill:** Refrigerate the tray 20–30 min. Heat the oven to 450°F and put a big pot of water on.
7. **Roast:** Roast the meatballs 10–12 min until browned. Broil for the last minute if they need more colour.
8. **Into the sauce:** Lift the onions out of the sauce and discard (or eat them). Taste for salt. Slide in the meatballs plus any browned bits from the pan, and simmer gently 15–20 min.
9. **Pasta:** Salt the water until it tastes like mild seawater. Cook the spaghetti until 1 min short of al dente. Save a mug of pasta water.
10. **Toss and serve:** In a separate pan or the empty pasta pot, toss the spaghetti with a few ladles of sauce and a splash of pasta water over medium heat until glossy and clinging. Plate, top with meatballs and more sauce, then Parmesan and torn burrata if using.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Why it tastes creamy:** The butter emulsifies into the tomato as it simmers. Keep the heat low so it stays silky rather than splitting greasy.
- **The onion is just for flavour:** It sweetens and rounds the sauce, then comes out. Don't chop it.
- **Delicate sauce, gentle proteins:** This sauce is mellow and sweet. Spicy fennel sausage crumbled in would take it over. Small, mild meatballs work with it.
- **Double the sauce:** The meatballs drink up sauce as they simmer. A single batch won't leave enough for the pasta.
- **Panade first, meat last:** Mixing the seasonings into the bread paste before the meat goes in spreads them evenly without overworking the meat.
- **Don't overmix:** Overworked meat turns tough and bouncy. Stop as soon as it comes together.
- **Always test-fry one:** It's the only way to know the seasoning is right before you roll 45 of them.
- **Fresh bread, not dry crumbs:** Dry breadcrumbs make dense meatballs. Soaked fresh bread keeps them tender.
- **Use fatty meat:** 80/20 beef plus pork. Lean meat makes dry meatballs.
- **Chill before cooking:** Cold meatballs hold their shape and brown better.
- **Roasting beats pan-frying for small ones:** You get browning on all sides without turning dozens of meatballs one by one. Pan-searing in batches gives a slightly better crust but takes longer and spatters.
- **Simmer, never boil:** A hard boil in the sauce toughens the meatballs. Keep it at a lazy bubble.
- **Toss the pasta separately:** Saucing the spaghetti in its own pan with pasta water gets it glossy without breaking up the meatballs.
- **Leftovers:** Sauce and meatballs keep 3–4 days in the fridge and freeze well for ~3 months. Store them separately from the pasta.

## Next Steps

Ways to push the meatballs further or build the night out.

### 1. Beef, pork & veal
- **What:** Swap half the beef for ground veal.
- **Why:** The classic Italian-American blend. Veal makes them softer and more delicate.

### 2. Make the meatballs ahead
- **What:** Roll and roast the meatballs a day ahead (or freeze the roasted ones). Rewarm them in the sauce for 20–25 min.
- **Why:** Takes most of the hands-on work off the day. Frozen meatballs go straight into the simmering sauce; just add ~10 min.

### 3. Chicken milanese as a second course
- **What:** Serve the pasta first, then thin breaded chicken cutlets with arugula and lemon.
- **Why:** The Italian primo-then-secondo way. The crunch contrasts with the silky spaghetti, and leftovers make great sandwiches.

### 4. Seared sausage on the side
- **What:** Brown whole Italian sausage links, slice them, and serve them next to the pasta. Don't crumble them into the sauce.
- **Why:** An easy no-rolling protein that keeps the sauce's flavour intact.

### 5. Cousin dishes: amatriciana and carbonara
- **What:** Two Roman spaghetti dishes built on guanciale and pecorino. Amatriciana is tomato-based. Carbonara gets its creaminess from egg yolks, cheese and pasta water.
- **Why:** The other two shortlisted options from this round. Good next spaghetti nights.
