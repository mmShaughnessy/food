# Orecchiette with Sausage & Broccolini

A Puglian classic, *orecchiette con cime di rapa e salsiccia*, made with broccolini instead of broccoli rabe. Browned sausage, garlic, anchovy and chilli bloomed in the sausage fat, then everything tossed with pasta water until glossy. Serves 4. About 40 minutes.

## Ingredients

**Greens**
- 1 lb (~450 g) broccolini (or broccoli rabe), cut bite-sized, thick stems split lengthways

**Sausage base**
- 2–3 tbsp olive oil, plus more to finish
- 1 lb (~450 g) Italian sausage, casings removed
- 4–5 garlic cloves, thinly sliced
- ~1 tsp anchovy paste (or 2–3 anchovy fillets)
- ½–1 tsp red chilli flakes

**Pasta**
- 1 lb (~450 g) orecchiette
- Kosher salt, for the water

**To finish**
- ~1 cup (~3 oz) Pecorino Romano, grated, plus more for the table
- Lemon (juice + zest), or a small splash of white or red wine vinegar
- Optional: ~1 cup breadcrumbs, toasted in olive oil until golden

## Steps

1. **Water on:** Bring a big pot of water to a boil and salt it until it tastes like mild seawater.
2. **Brown the sausage:** In a large skillet over medium-high, heat the olive oil. Crumble in the sausage in small pieces and leave it alone to brown hard, flipping once, ~6–8 min. No garlic yet.
3. **Blanch the greens:** Once the water boils, cook the broccolini 2–3 min until bright green and just tender. Scoop out with tongs or a slotted spoon. **Keep the water.**
4. **Pasta in:** Drop the orecchiette into the same water. Cook ~2 min less than the packet says (pull at ~9–10 min for an 11–13 min packet).
5. **Aromatics:** Turn the skillet to medium and push the sausage aside. Add the garlic, anchovy paste and chilli flakes to the fat and stir ~1 min until fragrant and the anchovy melts in. Don't let the garlic brown. Then turn the heat to low.
6. **Greens in the pan:** Add the blanched broccolini to the skillet so it warms through and soaks up the garlicky fat.
7. **Prep while it cooks:** Grate the pecorino, zest/halve the lemon, toast breadcrumbs if using, and set a mug by the stove.
8. **Save water, combine:** Scoop out a mug of pasta water, then move the pasta into the skillet. Add a good splash of the water.
9. **Emulsify:** Toss hard over medium heat 1–2 min until the sauce is glossy and coats everything. Add more water if it looks dry or oily.
10. **Finish & serve:** Off the heat, stir in the pecorino, a squeeze of lemon (or the splash of vinegar) and a drizzle of olive oil. Top with more cheese and the breadcrumbs. Serve right away.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Cook everything in one pot of water:** Blanching the greens first flavours the water the pasta then cooks in.
- **Sausage first, garlic later:** Garlic burns in the time it takes to brown sausage. Bloom it in the fat afterwards.
- **Actually brown the sausage:** Small crumbles, left alone, until crusty. They catch in the pasta's cups, and that crust is most of the flavour.
- **Don't crisp the garlic:** Brown garlic turns bitter. Stop once it's fragrant and soft.
- **Anchovy disappears:** It melts away completely and makes the dish taste deeper and more savoury, not fishy. Paste is easiest.
- **The emulsion matters most:** Starchy pasta water plus fat plus tossing is what makes it glossy instead of oily and dry.
- **Broccolini vs. rabe:** They're different vegetables. Rabe is leafy and bitter (turnip family). Broccolini is sweeter and milder, so add a bit more chilli and acid to make up for the lost bite.
- **Other subs if no rabe:** Mustard or turnip greens (closest bitterness, blanch ~1 min), regular broccoli plus a handful of bitter greens, or lacinato kale (2–3 min).
- **No lemon:** A small splash of white or red wine vinegar at the end gives the same brightness. Otherwise lean on extra pecorino and chilli.
- **Eat it immediately:** It's best hot while the sauce is glossy. As it cools, the pecorino tightens and the pasta goes stodgy.

## Next Steps

### 1. Pangrattato topping
- **What:** Toast breadcrumbs in olive oil with garlic and lemon zest until golden. Scatter over just before serving.
- **Why:** Puglia's "poor man's Parmesan." Adds crunch against the soft pasta.

### 2. Homemade fennel sausage
- **What:** Season 1 lb plain ground pork with crushed fennel seed, garlic, chilli flakes, black pepper, salt and a splash of white wine.
- **Why:** Brighter than shop sausage, and fennel is the classic pairing with bitter greens.

### 3. Turn up the heat
- **What:** Swap the flakes for chopped Calabrian chillies, or melt in a spoonful of 'nduja with the garlic.
- **Why:** Fruitier heat, or smoky, spicy richness.

### 4. Hand-made orecchiette
- **What:** Semolina and warm water dough. Drag small discs across the board with a knife and flip each over your thumb.
- **Why:** A weekend project in the spirit of the bolognese. Chewier, rougher pasta that holds the sauce.

### 5. Cousin dish: pasta alla norcina
- **What:** Sausage, onion, white wine, cream, nutmeg and pecorino on rigatoni. Optional black truffle.
- **Why:** The rich, creamy Umbrian counterpart to this bright, bitter dish.
