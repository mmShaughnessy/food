# One-Pan Creamy Gnocchi with Sausage

A weeknight one-skillet dinner adapted from Salt & Lavender. Browned sausage and onion, a splash of white wine, then tomatoes and cream. Shop-bought gnocchi goes in raw and cooks right in the sauce, and its starch thickens it. Serves 4. About 30 minutes.

Source: [Salt & Lavender – One Pan Creamy Gnocchi with Sausage](https://www.saltandlavender.com/one-pan-creamy-gnocchi-with-sausage/)

## Ingredients

**Sausage base**
- 1 lb (~450 g) Italian sausage, casings removed (mild or hot)
- ½ small onion, finely chopped
- 4 garlic cloves, minced

**Sauce**
- ⅓ cup (~80 ml) dry white wine (e.g. sauvignon blanc)
- 1 can (14 oz / ~400 g) diced tomatoes, with juices
- 1 cup (~240 ml) heavy cream

**Gnocchi**
- 1 lb (~450 g) shop-bought potato gnocchi, uncooked (shelf-stable or refrigerated)

**To finish**
- ½ cup (~1.5 oz) Parmesan, freshly grated, plus more for the table
- Handful of fresh basil, torn
- Salt and black pepper
- Optional: red chilli flakes, a couple of handfuls of baby spinach

## Steps

1. **Prep first:** Chop the onion, mince the garlic, open the tomatoes, grate the Parmesan and measure the wine and cream. It moves fast once it starts.
2. **Brown the sausage & onion:** In a large, deep skillet with a lid, over medium-high, crumble in the sausage and add the onion. Cook 7–10 min, breaking it up, until the sausage is browned and the onion soft.
3. **Garlic:** Stir in the garlic for ~30 sec, just until fragrant.
4. **Deglaze:** Pour in the wine and scrape up the browned bits. Let it bubble ~1 min to cook off the raw alcohol.
5. **Sauce + gnocchi in:** Stir in the tomatoes with their juices, the cream and the raw gnocchi.
6. **Covered simmer:** Once it bubbles, cover, drop the heat to medium and cook 5 min.
7. **Uncovered finish:** Lid off, stir, and cook a few more minutes until the gnocchi are tender and the sauce has thickened. Stir in spinach now if using, until wilted.
8. **Finish & serve:** Off the heat, stir in the Parmesan and basil. Season with salt, pepper (and chilli flakes if you like). Serve right away.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Don't boil the gnocchi:** Cooking them raw in the sauce is the whole trick. They release starch that thickens it.
- **Shop-bought only:** Homemade gnocchi are too soft and will fall apart in the simmering sauce.
- **Use a pan with a lid:** The covered stage steams the gnocchi through. A deep skillet avoids spatter.
- **Sausage fat is flavour:** Unless there's a pool of grease, leave it in. It carries the onion and garlic.
- **Sauce too thin:** Simmer uncovered a little longer. **Too thick or catching:** splash in cream or water and lower the heat. Hot stoves catch fast once the gnocchi starch is in.
- **No wine:** Use ½ cup chicken broth plus ½ tsp Dijon mustard.
- **Heat:** Hot Italian sausage or chilli flakes.
- **Lighter cream:** Half-and-half works but is less rich and can split with the acidic tomatoes. Heavy cream is safest.
- **Sausage amount is flexible:** Anything around 1 lb works with standard package sizes.
- **Leftovers:** Fridge, covered, 3–4 days. Reheat gently on low with a splash of cream or wine. Doesn't freeze well; the cream sauce and gnocchi texture suffer.

## Next Steps

### 1. Crisp the gnocchi
- **What:** Pan-fry the gnocchi in butter or oil until golden first, set aside, then fold them back into the finished sauce.
- **Why:** Crispy outside, pillowy inside, instead of all-soft. Costs one extra step but no extra pan.

### 2. Go full rosé with vodka
- **What:** Swap the wine for a splash of vodka and add a spoonful of tomato paste, browned in the fat before deglazing.
- **Why:** Deeper, sweeter tomato flavour; a vodka-sauce riff on the same method.

### 3. Greens and heat
- **What:** Stir in spinach or chopped kale at the end, and use hot sausage or a spoonful of Calabrian chilli.
- **Why:** Cuts through the richness and makes it more of a one-bowl meal.

### 4. Homemade fennel sausage
- **What:** Same seasoning as the orecchiette notes: ground pork with crushed fennel, garlic, chilli, pepper, salt and a splash of wine.
- **Why:** Brighter, more controllable flavour than shop sausage.

### 5. Cousin dish: pasta alla norcina
- **What:** Sausage, onion, white wine, cream, nutmeg and pecorino on rigatoni. No tomato.
- **Why:** The white, creamy version of this dish. Easy to compare side by side.
