# Pappardelle Bolognese

This is a classic Bologna-style ragù: pancetta and soffritto, beef and pork, white wine and milk, a long, slow simmer, then a splash of cream at the end. Serve on pappardelle (or tagliatelle). Serves 6.

## Ingredients

**Base**
- 2 tbsp olive oil
- 2 tbsp unsalted butter
- 100 g (~3.5 oz) pancetta, finely chopped

**Soffritto**
- 1 medium onion, finely diced
- 1 medium carrot, finely diced
- 2 celery stalks, finely diced

**Meat**
- 500 g (~1 lb) ground beef (chuck, ~20% fat)
- 250 g (~½ lb) ground pork
- 1½ tsp kosher salt

**Sauce**
- 1 cup dry white wine
- 1 cup whole milk
- Pinch of nutmeg
- 2 tbsp tomato paste
- 400 g (~14 oz) tomato passata or crushed tomatoes
- 1 cup beef or chicken stock
- ½ tsp black pepper, plus more salt to taste
- ~¼ cup heavy cream

**To serve**
- 500 g (~1 lb) pappardelle or tagliatelle
- Knob of butter
- 50 g (~½ cup) Parmigiano-Reggiano, grated

## Steps

1. **Render:** In a heavy Dutch oven over medium heat, warm the oil and butter. Add the pancetta and cook ~5 min, until the fat renders and it just starts to crisp.
2. **Soffritto:** Add the onion, carrot and celery. Cook gently ~10 min, stirring often, until very soft and sweet but not browned.
3. **Brown the meat:** Raise to medium-high. Add the beef, pork and salt, break it up, and cook ~12 min, until the liquid cooks off and the meat browns in spots.
4. **Wine:** Pour in the wine, scrape up the browned bits, and simmer ~5 min until almost evaporated.
5. **Milk:** Add the milk and nutmeg and simmer ~8 min until absorbed.
6. **Tomato:** Stir in the tomato paste and cook 1–2 min, then add the passata and stock. Bring to a bare simmer.
7. **Slow simmer:** Cook uncovered on the lowest heat for ~3 hours, with only an occasional bubble, stirring every 20–30 min. Add a splash of water or stock if it gets too dry.
8. **Cream:** In the last 10 min, stir in the cream. Season with pepper and more salt.
9. **Pasta:** Boil the pasta in well-salted water until 1 min short of al dente. Save a mug of pasta water before draining.
10. **Toss and serve:** Toss the pasta in the sauce over low heat with a splash of pasta water and a knob of butter until it clings. Top with Parmesan.

## Nuances & Tips

- **Chop the soffritto very fine:** A food processor pulsed to a coarse paste works great. It melts into the sauce.
- **Actually brown the meat:** Let the liquid cook off before stirring much, so you get browned bits instead of grey, steamed meat. Browning is where a lot of the flavour comes from.
- **Don't skip the milk:** It tenderises the meat, softens the tomato's acidity and gives the sauce a velvety texture.
- **Cream goes in late:** Add it only in the last 10 min. A long cook breaks it down and it loses its richness. ~¼ cup makes the sauce richer and paler without turning it into a cream sauce.
- **Go light on tomato:** Real ragù is a meat sauce with some tomato, not a tomato sauce with meat.
- **Visual cues:** It's ready when thick and glossy and the fat starts pooling at the edges, whatever the clock says.
- **Shorter simmer:** Edible after ~1½ hours, and most of the way there at ~2 hours, but the meat stays firmer and the flavour is brighter and less mellow. Use a wider pan to reduce faster.
- **Resting:** If serving the same day, rest it off the heat with the lid on for an hour or so, then rewarm gently. A few hours in the fridge adds little. The real "better the next day" effect needs an overnight rest.
- **Skimming fat:** Chilling hardens the fat on top so you can lift some off if it's greasy.
- **Pasta shape:** Wide, flat pasta holds the sauce far better than spaghetti. Finish it in the sauce with pasta water so it turns glossy.
- **Storage:** Leftovers keep a few days in the fridge and freeze well for ~3 months.

## Next Steps

### 1. Make it a day ahead
- **What:** Cook the sauce through the slow simmer, cool it, and refrigerate overnight. Rewarm gently, add the cream in the last 10 min, and toss with fresh pasta.
- **Why:** Overnight, the flavours meld and the meat soaks up the sauce. It also lets you skim the hardened fat.

### 2. Ragù bianco (white bolognese)
- **What:** Leave out the tomato paste and passata entirely: just meat, soffritto, wine, milk, cream and stock.
- **Why:** It's very rich and comforting, a different take on the same base.

### 3. Pressure-cooker shortcut
- **What:** After the tomato step, cook ~45 min at high pressure, then simmer uncovered 10–15 min to thicken.
- **Why:** It gets very close to the 3-hour version when time is short. Brown the meat hard to make up for the shorter cook.
